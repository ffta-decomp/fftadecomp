# Battle group map/reduce recovery

The current campaign concentrates every implementation lane on one battle
action, effect, resolution and turn-continuation group. This changes the next
claim, not ownership of work already in progress: finish and deliver current
claimed functions and collect running builds before switching. Preserve their
original clocks, failed causes, evidence and remaining budgets.

The [ordered system roadmap](game-recovery-priorities.md#system-order-and-remaining-contracts)
governs later groups. Keep one active business group at a time, with concurrent
map units inside it. Group reduce records accepted/integrated members, real
dependency interfaces and unresolved native ranges. An empty queue or one failed
member is not group completion. Do not silently skip residuals or open several
independent system campaigns; record a genuine group-level blocker for priority
resolution. Required dependencies from later groups may be recovered early and
retain their real owner and unique recovery credit.

## Shared group and map work

Use the current assignment's shared group packet and the existing
`config/battle-action-chain-01b.json` (implementation-checkout reference) as source-backed
entry points. They are discovery anchors, not proof that every reachable helper
is game logic or ready for rewriting. Keep native method/task identities intact;
record the campaign group and dependency relationship separately.

Each actor autonomously claims a different complete original method through the
existing CLI. Prefer small methods whose contracts and immediate dependencies
are understood; progress bottom-up through the same consumer chain. Small-first
is an ordering preference, not a byte threshold or permission to split a shared
frame. Reuse a proven source/compiler structure across applicable siblings.

For every encountered dependency, reuse or record one of these dispositions:

| Dependency | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Existing completed C or eligible mixed implementation | Reuse its pinned body and typed interface; no new migration credit. |
| Untranslated necessary dependency, including a simple generic helper | Establish its actual caller/callee contract and history, then claim and rewrite it before expanding the consumer where practical. Keep its true semantic owner; a generic helper need not become game policy to qualify. Another actor may claim it; never duplicate a live implementation. |
| Complex generic runtime, scheduler, memory, graphics, audio, hardware or other low-level mechanism | Describe ABI, widths, layout, effects, ordering, clobbers and uncertainties. Bind its existing real implementation; do not rewrite the body in this campaign. |
| Unknown ownership or contract | Resolve only the bounded question needed by the battle consumer. Do not relabel by name or caller alone, or launch a global classification pass. |

Being outside the active business group or carrying an unknown/engine label does
not itself block a necessary dependency. Prove the native boundary, required
input/output effect, argument/return widths, state access and concrete battle
consumer edge. For a simple helper this contract can be sufficient even when
its broader game meaning remains unknown. Complex low-level implementations
remain interface-only. Record the evidence behind that distinction; code size
or a directory name alone is insufficient. Unknown ABI or effects remain actual
blockers until resolved, never invented declarations or replacement stubs.

Existing ASM dependencies remain callable while their C migration is pending.
Bottom-up ordering must not become a requirement to rewrite every transitive
callee before compiling anything. Cycles and shared-frame entries stay explicit;
retain typed bindings and choose another independent map unit when blocked.

Reuse source-hash-bound dependency facts in the existing task spec, round or
lesson, rather than duplicating long reports. Record the caller/call site,
callee native identity, disposition, contract reference and implementation state.
Do not introduce a competing claim database or reserve a whole group to one actor.

## Reduce and delivery

Submit every reached per-function local gate immediately. Group completion,
lessons and integration do not delay a valid local delivery. Fixed O2, complete
original-range proof, the existing cause budgets and independent mechanical or
semantic gates remain unchanged.

At safe boundaries, a different actor consumes frozen packages and reconciles
shared interfaces and dependency identities. Main alone combines accepted
packages into coherent production batches, confirms linked bytes and performs
the required image gate. Reduce must not rewrite another actor's live source,
merge private drafts, rerun unchanged banks or build a full ROM per small method.
An unresolved method is reported separately and does not hold unrelated ready
members hostage. Existing out-of-group frozen deliveries are still collected.

Measure deduplicated matched original ASM bytes for the group, methods delivered,
dependency edges closed, interface-only boundaries and remaining unknowns.
Keep local, independent and integrated gates separate. Compare absolute completed
hours and complete method latency, charging discovery, failures, coordination and
reduce time. Interface descriptions and existing implementations earn no new
rewritten-byte credit. No historical global percentage becomes a battle-group
completion denominator until that group's native ranges are established.
